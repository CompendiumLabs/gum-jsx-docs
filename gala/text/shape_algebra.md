# Shape Algebra

This figure treats gum elements as first-class math atoms. A [MathText](/docs/MathText) row normally holds LaTeX strings and math elements, but an ordinary `Element` can be dropped in as well, where it counts as a single ordinary atom and is sized and spaced like one. Here that is used three times: a hand-built square sits on the right-hand side of a series, a `Circle` (the unit-aspect [Ellipse](/docs/Ellipse)) is the numerator of a [Frac](/docs/Frac) under a [Sqrt](/docs/Sqrt), and two little matrices of colored swatches show a transpose you can see.

The nesting is what makes it work. Because the math constructors are just elements, the inline shapes participate in the layout at every level: the radical picks a surd tall enough to cover the fraction that contains the circle, the square [Bracket](/docs/Bracket) stretches to the height of the swatch [MathArray](/docs/MathArray) inside it, and the [SupSub](/docs/SupSub) hangs its `\mathsf{T}` off the top corner of the bracket. Nothing about the shapes needs to know it is inside an equation.

`HalfSquare` is the one piece of real construction. It is a [Group](/docs/Group) with `aspect={1}` holding six rectangles placed by explicit `rect` coordinates that halve at each step, colored along a `palette` from blue to red, with a bare [Rect](/docs/Rect) on top for the outline. The `Swatch` component wraps a rounded `Square` (the unit-aspect **Rect**) in a padded [Box](/docs/Box) so the cells of the **MathArray** keep some air between them, and `SwatchMatrix` is a plain function that builds the bracketed array from a list of colors, so the two sides of the transpose differ only in the order of their arguments.

The three rows are separate **MathText** expressions stacked in an `even` [VStack](/docs/Stack) inside a [TitleFrame](/docs/TitleFrame), which gives each row the same height and centers it.
